The owl's uneven ears

An owl's ears are uneven. That is the whole trick.

A barn owl drops onto a mouse it has never seen — in the dark, under snow. Not because its eyes are extraordinary. Because its two ear openings point in different directions: the left one aimed down, the right one aimed up.

Almost every animal with two ears can tell left from right. Sound reaches the nearer ear a fraction sooner, and that head start is the direction. But it says nothing about height — a mouse above you and a mouse below you arrive at both ears alike. Uneven ears are how the owl gets the second axis, and they are why it can hit what it cannot see.

So: you are the owl, and it is dark. You have two readings, and no numbers to work out. Each one shows what you are hearing and what you would hear from where you are aiming. Move your aim until the two line up — which is exactly what an owl is doing when it turns its head towards a sound.

What just happened

With uneven ears you could always find it. Two readings, two unknowns — line both up and there is exactly one place the mouse can be. That is not a knack you developed. It is arithmetic the shape of the owl's head does for it.

Level the ears and the loudness reading keeps working. It still swings, you can still line it up, and it still tells you when you have matched it — because the ears are still on opposite sides of a head. What it has stopped doing is saying anything about height. The reading at the top of the field is identical to the reading at the bottom. Not weaker. Identical.

So the instrument told you that you were on target, and you were not. That is the part worth keeping: a reading that responds is not the same as a reading that knows. Nothing looked broken, nothing got noisier, and you were guessing the height every single time. The scoreboard is where you found out.

Aiming an ear is what turns it into a measuring instrument. Two ears pointed the same way are one instrument used twice; point them apart and the pair answers a question neither could answer alone. The owl's skull is lopsided for exactly one reason, and it is the reason it eats.
