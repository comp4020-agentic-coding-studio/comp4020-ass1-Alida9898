A barn owl takes a mouse it never sees — under snow, in the dark. That much is famous. What gets left out is why: the bird is built crooked on purpose.

Difficulty: human.

You have been hunting with a barn owl's ears — offset vertically, left aimed down, right aimed up. That is the only reason the side gauge could tell you a height at all.

They are gone. You have your own ears now: level, like every human's. Same dark, same mouse, same left-to-right timing.

Watch the side gauge.

And what about you?

The levelled ears in the hunt were not a thought experiment. They were yours — you have had them your whole life, and you have never once placed a sound by height by comparing them.

Look at the marks on the field. The green sit in a band at whatever height you kept guessing; the red fill in everywhere else. Left and right were perfect all along. Every miss was vertical.
