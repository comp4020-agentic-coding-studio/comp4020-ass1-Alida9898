# Assignment 1

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was not a technical one. It was deciding what to make, and then
deciding what it would not be.

I had a pile of ideas and none of them felt clearly right — some were too big for a
week, some I did not think I could pull off. This one did not feel like an obvious
winner either. What changed things was committing to it anyway and then cutting it
down: not owls in general, not their eyes or their necks, only the ears. Once the
edges were fixed, everything after that got easier, because every later question
had something to be measured against.

The part I liked most was the hunt itself — building an interaction that makes you
feel the difference instead of reading about it.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I think the ability that matters most now is independent judgement. The AI is right
most of the time, and that is exactly why you have to keep asking where it might
not be. Twice this week I pushed back on an explanation it was confident about, and
twice it turned out to be wrong — not because I knew the physics better, but
because the answer did not match what having two ears actually feels like.

That is the part I do not think a model takes over. A lot of what makes something
work is a feeling rather than a reason, and you only get at it by talking to the
person who has to use it. In a time when the tooling is this strong, being the one
who asks "is that actually true?" is worth more than being the one who writes the
code.
